# PITFIGHTER texture set

Image files for the stage floors and walls, and the recipe for the fighters'
skin, cloth, limbs and face. The renderer is to load these instead of the
procedural surfaces in `src/render/textures.ts`; the procedural surfaces stay
as the fallback.

The fighters' files are not here yet. The first set was made for the old
roster of 21 devils and was removed when the roster was replaced by the twelve
fighters of Old Sorrow (`docs/characters.md`). Their recipes are in `spec.json`,
ready to render; `docs/texture-handoff.md` has the brief.

26 files, about 3.5 MiB, in total, against a 25 MB budget. The old set with its
fighters was 152 files and 11.56 MiB.

## What is here

```
manifest.json          one entry per file: model, licence, prompt, seed, workflow, post steps
spec.json              the prompt and seed for every surface - the source of truth for the set
fighters/<slug>/skin.webp      512x512   seamless colour, the hide
fighters/<slug>/skin_h.webp    512x512   seamless height, greyscale, white is raised
fighters/<slug>/cloth.webp     512x512   seamless colour, the clothes
fighters/<slug>/cloth_h.webp   512x512   seamless height for the cloth (not in the
                                          original brief, but the renderer bakes a
                                          bump map for cloth as well as for skin)
fighters/<slug>/face.webp      512x512   RGBA, the face mask on a transparent background
fighters/<slug>/limb.webp      512x512   seamless colour, the craft the limbs are made in
fighters/<slug>/limb_h.webp    512x512   seamless height for the limbs
stages/<stage>/<surface>.webp        1024x1024 seamless colour
stages/<stage>/<surface>_h.webp     1024x1024 seamless height
stages/hell-toilet/basalt_glow.webp  1024x1024 greyscale, white where the lava cracks glow
workflows/<name>.json        the ComfyUI graphs that produced the set, API format
contact/<slug>.png           2x2 tiling preview plus the face, for review
contact/<stage>.png          2x2 tiling preview of a stage's surfaces
props/<asset>*.png           the 3D stage's approved references: the alpha cutout
                            and the four-view turnaround sheet
characters/<slug>.jpg        the character's concept art, for the 3D stage
```

`props/` and `characters/` are not part of the generated material set. They
are the references the 3D stage works from - the alpha cutout and the
four-view turnaround that a human reviewer approved, and the character art
they were drawn for - and they carry no entry in `manifest.json`, because
that manifest records how a tile is reproduced and these are not tiles. The
asset they belong to is recorded in `data/asset_manifest.json`.

`<surface>` is the id of the procedural recipe it replaces: `basalt`, `dirt`,
`concrete`, `roof`, `planks`, `stone`.

## How it was made

Everything was generated locally on the RX 9070 XT with ComfyUI on native
ROCm - no cloud, no reference images, no material from anywhere else.

- **ComfyUI** 0.33.0 with PyTorch 2.9.1+rocm7.2.1, AMD arch gfx1201.
- **FLUX.1 [schnell]** in fp8 (Apache-2.0), 4 steps, guidance 3.5, euler/simple.
  `flux1-schnell-fp8.safetensors` comes from `Comfy-Org/Flux-1-Schnell`, which
  republishes the Apache-2.0 weights without a gate. The two text encoders
  (`clip_l`, `t5xxl_fp8_e4m3fn`) and the `ae` VAE were already on the machine.
- Every surface is rendered at 1024x1024 and downscaled with Lanczos.
- One ComfyUI instance serves both the 9070 XT and the machine's iGPU, and the
  order they are enumerated in changes between boots. The server is therefore
  always started with `--cuda-device 1`, and the selected device is confirmed
  through `/system_stats` before a batch runs.

The first set was generated by these ComfyUI graphs, each assembled from
reusable fragments:

| workflow | what it does |
|---|---|
| `workflows/skin.json` | the skins (and the limbs, which are materials too) |
| `workflows/cloth.json` | the cloths |
| `workflows/face.json` | the faces |
| `workflows/stage.json` | the 6 stage surfaces |
| `workflows/seam.json` | the inpaint that heals the cross a rolled tile leaves |

### Making a tile

A diffusion model does not produce a tile that repeats. The tile is made in
four steps:

1. **Generate** at 1024x1024, `denoise 1.0`.
2. **Pick a render that tiles.** How badly a render fails to tile depends on
   the seed: some come out with a hard split down the middle, others with a
   strong gradient across it, and the roll puts that straight onto the tile's
   border. So the materials that measure worst are rendered again with other
   seeds, and whichever render tiles best is the one that is kept. The seed
   that was used is recorded in `spec.json` under `seed_overrides`.
3. **Roll the image by half.** A cyclic shift by half the width makes the
   tile's borders continuous - the left edge becomes the original's centre
   column's left neighbour - because the two edges are now adjacent pixels of
   the same render. It leaves one cross down the middle, where the render's
   opposite edges meet.
4. **Heal the cross.** That cross is the only discontinuity left, and it is
   exactly as rough as the render's own borders were, so FLUX inpaints it:
   a feathered cross-shaped mask over the middle of the rolled tile, and the
   fill is blended back over the rolled original so the tile's borders stay
   pixel-exact.

The inpaint runs at `denoise 0.6`, not at 1.0. At 1.0 the model does not
continue the surrounding material, it paints a *different* material into the
band - on the red devil hide that came out as a bright vermilion cross, on
basalt as a near-black one. At 0.6 it continues the material, and the band
matches the rest of the tile in colour and in texture.

But 0.6 continues the material too well on a satin or a drape, whose folds
are large compared with the tile: the band comes out flat and the cross reads
as a cross. So the band is also inpainted at `denoise 0.75`, which paints the
fold back in, and the two are measured against each other and the better one
is kept. On a material where the model would rather paint something else,
0.75 is the worse of the two and 0.6 stays.

That is still not enough on a satin or a drape. Their folds are two hundred
pixels across where the band is thirty-two pixels across, and a band that
narrow cannot repaint a fold - it can only blur the edge of one, and the step
is still there at the bottom of the blur. So a tile that is still rough after
the narrow heal gets a third heal: the same 32 pixel band at `denoise 0.9`,
over a few seeds, and the best of those is measured against the first two.
Four of the 48 tiles needed it, and they are exactly the satin and drape
folds. The stage can widen the band as well, to 80 and then 160 pixels either
side of the middle, for a tile that 0.9 does not fix - none of the four needed
it, but the band is there.

The band is 16 pixels either side of the middle and the blend that lays the
healed band back over the rolled original ramps out to 28 pixels either side,
so the blend is 56 pixels across and the band is 32: the blend is wider than
the band, because a blend narrower than the band lets the edge of the band
through as a step of its own. Both numbers are measured from the middle, and
the mask that carries the band is in the ComfyUI input folder, so the widths
can be checked rather than taken on trust.

The manifest records which of the four candidates each file is and, for the
repaired ones, the band width, the inpaint strength and the seed, so the file
can be reproduced from the manifest alone.

### How a tile is measured

Every tile is measured twice, because the two failure modes are independent:

- **Seam score.** The largest of the four boundary differences (the wrap on
  each axis, the cross on each axis) divided by the median difference between
  neighbouring pixels inside the tile; 1.0 means every boundary is as smooth
  as the material itself.
- **Band colour drift.** The difference between the mean colour of the middle
  band and the mean colour of the four quadrants, in 0-255 units.

The seam score alone is not enough. A band that is perfectly smooth but the
wrong colour passes it and is still glaringly obvious, which is exactly what
happened when the inpaint ran at `denoise 1.0`. So the healed tile is only
used when it is smoother *and* its colour has not drifted.

The score also has to be read with care. The wrap is the rolled tile's own
border, and the roll makes that continuous by construction, so a high wrap
ratio usually means a strong feature - a fold, a crack - that continues into
the next tile rather than a seam. The cross is the real seam, because that is
where the render's opposite borders meet.

And the measurement is made on the file that ships, not on the image that goes
into it. The tile is measured after the downscale to 512x512 and after the
WebP encode: the downscale takes the high frequency out of the material,
which lowers the difference between neighbouring pixels inside the tile, and
the encoder rings on a hard edge. Which of the four candidates - the rolled
tile, the band healed at 0.6, the band healed at 0.75, the band healed by the
repair - is the best one changes with the size, so it has to be measured at
the size that ships.

### Height maps

The height map is the colour image's luminance after a high pass, lightly
blurred and quantised to 32 levels, saved as a lossless greyscale WebP. The
high pass keeps the surface relief and throws away the lighting gradient, so
a flat-lit material still gives a usable bump map.

### Faces

The face is rendered front-on on a black background. The mask is cut by
flooding the background from the edge of the frame, so dark features inside
the face - eyes, mouth, nostrils - stay opaque and only the background
goes. The flood has to be tight: the renders sit on a black background, and a
loose flood walks through the dark horn above the face and into the shadow
that horn throws across the cheek, and the face comes out with a hole in it.
The small holes the flood leaves inside the face are filled, but only the
small ones, because the hole between a pair of horns is not small and the sky
has to stay sky. The silhouette is then mirrored about the head's *own* axis,
because the skull it is projected onto is symmetric, and a head the model
placed off centre must not end up off centre on the other side.

The crop is the awkward part. The model draws horns and ears, and the game
draws those as separate meshes, but the head is not the face: the blob that
comes out of the flood fill includes the horns, the ears, and for one
fighter a painted mask. So the frame is not sized from the blob but from an
eroded copy of it - the erosion is as deep as it can be while the mass that
is left still looks like a head, so a big horn goes and a small head
survives - and the frame is anchored on the **brow line** rather than on the
top of that mass.

The brow line is measured on the run of the silhouette that holds the
symmetry axis. A horn stands off to the side and the sky between a pair of
horns is a gap, so the run on the axis is the face itself, and the row where
that run first reaches its widest is the brow. Anchoring the frame there
keeps the forehead - and the horn bases standing on it - out of the picture,
which is what "brow to chin" asks for. A head with tusks has a mass that is
not on the axis, and a frame built on that lands on the tusk, so a mass that
is off the axis is rejected.

Because that is a set of rules and not a measurement, several frames are
tried and the one that measures best as a face is kept: a face fills the
frame, sits in the middle of it and has structure in it, where a crop that
landed on a cheek or on a horn fills the frame too but is flat. A horn is
dark and hard, so a crop that holds one of them has *more* structure in it
and scores better on every other count; the one thing that tells the two
apart is that a horn arrives in the top corner as a shape of its own, so the
rows at the top of the frame come in more than one piece where a face comes
in one. The matte is then resized to 512x512 with the alpha premultiplied
first, so the soft edge of the mask does not pick up a halo.

## Regenerating

`spec.json` has the prompt and the seed for every surface. The graphs in
`workflows/` are the API-format ComfyUI graphs that produced this set, with
the prompts already filled in, so a single file can be dropped back into
ComfyUI's API endpoint to reproduce one surface exactly.

The tooling that drove the generation lives outside the repository, in
`C:\comfy\pitfighter-textures`, because it is machine specific: it knows
where ComfyUI is installed and which models are on disk.

## Notes for whoever wires this up

- Every material is seamless in both directions. The contact sheets in
  `contact/` are the 2x2 tiling check; the fighter sheets also carry the
  face in the bottom-left panel.
- The textures are painted in their final colours, so turn the colour tint
  off for a textured fighter, as the brief says.
- `skin.webp` and `cloth.webp` are colour only; `*_h.webp` are the matching
  bump maps and are greyscale (white is raised).
- `face.webp` is RGBA and is meant to be projected onto the front of the
  skull. It is cropped to brow-to-chin, with no hair and no neck. A few of
  them still carry a small piece of horn or an ear at the edge of the frame -
  see Known limitations for which, and how big.
- `stages/hell-toilet/basalt_glow.webp` is the emissive map: white only in the
  cracks that the lava runs through.

## Known limitations

Nothing in this set is known to be unfinished. The two things that were open
when the first version of the set was delivered are closed.

**Every material tile's cross seam measures below 2.5.** The cross is the
seam the gate measures: the tile has four boundaries, the wrap on each axis
and the cross down the middle, and the wrap is continuous by construction
because the tile is rolled, so the cross is the only one that can be a
fault. The four that were over it - the satin and drape folds, whose scale
is close to the size of the 512x512 tile - were repaired with a third heal
at a stronger inpaint. The worst of them,
`fighters/brother-blaze/cloth.webp`, came down from 4.52 to 0.63. The worst
tile in the set is now `fighters/fresh-soul-kevin/skin.webp` at 2.46, which
is a smooth pale skin: the cross there is a soft change of shading across
the middle of the tile rather than a step, and the ratio is high because the
material itself is smooth enough that the step is large next to it.

**The faces are brow to chin.** The frames are anchored on the brow line,
measured on the run of the silhouette that holds the symmetry axis - a horn
stands off that axis, so the run is the face itself - and the top of the
frame is the brow, so the horn bases that stood on the forehead stay out of
the picture.

That is not the same as saying that no face carries a horn or an ear. The
crop cannot promise it, and it does not: a horn that rises from below the
brow, or an ear at the temple, is inside the frame, and where the model
fused a horn to the skull in the silhouette there is nothing to crop it
away with. Measured on the delivered files, 18 of the 21 masks are a single
connected piece. `gutter-gus` carries one piece of 494 pixels, `rage-reaper`
one of 351, and `sergeant-sulphur` thirteen - its jaw plate, and the horn
and ear bits around the head. Those are the only horn and ear fragments left
in the set, and they are small.

Two things are worth knowing rather than fixing. The face crop has to be
picked by measurement rather than by rule, because the model draws horns and
ears on every devil; the only thing that tells a crop of the face from a
crop of a horn is that a horn arrives in the top corner as a shape of its
own. And the background flood has to be tight: a loose one walks through the
dark horn above a face and into the shadow the horn throws across the cheek,
and the face comes out with a hole in it.
